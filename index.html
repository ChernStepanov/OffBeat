      How OffBeat finds a pixel out of step

      NISAR has only a few months of history, so a pixel's own past is too short to compare against. OffBeat compares it with its neighbours instead: every pixel of the same ESA WorldCover class within a few kilometres.

      If all the rice paddies around scatter alike and one drops sharply in HV, that pixel gets a z-score against its peers. A classifier then reads the shape of the curve and names the style: a drop, a flood jump, a slow drift or a step.

      The optical pane on the right follows the map, so every finding can be checked against a picture.
