# OffBeat — submission draft

**Challenge:** Dancing with the SARs. The selected challenge and the team's registered event still need confirmation on the official project page.

**One sentence:** Explore and hear how prepared NASA–ISRO NISAR radar measurements differ from nearby samples of the same historical land-cover class.

**Problem and audience:** Radar observations can reveal changes through clouds and at night, but the signal is difficult to interpret. OffBeat is an exploratory prototype for learners and researchers; its potential benefit has not been evaluated with users.

**What we built:** A browser app plots real L-band NISAR GCOV HH/HV series at sparse locations in seven prepared sites. It compares samples with at least ten eligible nearby same-class WorldCover 2021 samples, excluding the selected sample, and highlights exploratory departures. Some sites have as few as two acquisition dates. Users can inspect maps, quality-filtered GUNW line-of-sight results where available, and hear a designed mapping of measurements. Sound and candidate labels are not independent event detectors. Causes, areas, prediction accuracy and vertical subsidence rates are not established.

**What the data says:** [`scripts/findings.py`](https://github.com/ChernStepanov/OffBeat/blob/master/scripts/findings.py) tests the measured samples and the page opens with the results ([data/findings.json](https://github.com/ChernStepanov/OffBeat/blob/master/data/findings.json)). In Mexico City, 114 of 1,003 samples drift steadily away from their same-class neighbours over 29 dates (Kendall trend test on peer z, Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate 5%; correlated dates can overstate significance, so this is a ranking of candidates). Off-beat samples there cluster spatially (join-count permutation test, p = 0.007), while at five other sites they scatter like noise. The peer band holds 92-94% of measurements against the 95% it is designed for, typical 12-day noise is 1.2-1.9 dB, and scene-wide shifts such as -5.1 dB in Mexico on 22 January 2026 are separated from local change. HH and HV flag mostly different samples (only 5-28% of off-beat samples depart in both), so the two polarisations are complementary: HH reacts to surface roughness, moisture and double-bounce from buildings or water, HV to volume scattering in vegetation. Every finding carries labelled hypotheses about its physical drivers, such as construction, canopy change, groundwater-driven compaction under Mexico City, moisture or processing updates. Playing a sample animates the map through the acquisition dates in time with the sound. A Khimki site was removed after failing physical plausibility checks.

**An example:** In the Itupiranga, Pará window on 28 September 2026, median HV is −12.01 dB for 575 historical tree-cover samples and −15.55 dB for 449 grassland samples. The difference describes these selected observations; it is not a biomass estimate or a confirmed forest-loss event. Six native 20 m GCOV windows also support a bounded retrospective dense experiment: one four-cell candidate met a post-hoc rule, with no matching records in the checked local alert/fire subsets. Incomplete archives and post-hoc selection prevent conclusions about event absence, cause, area or recall. See [science review](https://github.com/ChernStepanov/OffBeat/blob/master/docs/science-review.md) and [external audit](https://github.com/ChernStepanov/OffBeat/blob/master/docs/amazon-external-audit-v2.md).

**Independent context and limits:** Quality-filtered Mexico OPERA DISP-S1 and Amazon OPERA RTC-S1 observations provide separate C-band context; dates, geometry and reference conditions differ from NISAR. For the common window, GNSS station MMX1 has a fitted vertical rate of -28.9 ± 1.0 cm/year, while OPERA DISP-S1 at a sample 20 m away has a relative line-of-sight fit of -4.8 ± 4.5 cm/year. These are different components and reference frames; neither the cause of the difference nor the stability of the radar reference is established. An experimental GNSS-tied layer assumes vertical-only station motion, a 29–46° incidence angle, and a constant rate beyond the GNSS observation window; a single anchor is not independent validation and the result is not a validated subsidence map. Formal slope errors do not capture all systematic uncertainties. Dated Sentinel-2 optical previews are available for prepared sites where indicated in their indexes. Some dates share an image; cloud, shadow, snow and missing data remain masked, and an unavailable date is explicit. Esri context mosaics have varying dates and are not date-matched validation.

**NASA data used:** NASA–ISRO NISAR provisional GCOV and GUNW products via ASF DAAC, NASA GIBS browse coverage, and NASA OPERA products. NASA FIRMS appears as source context where identified in the retained records. GIBS browse colours show acquisition coverage, not calibrated backscatter or a global event map. [Granule IDs, dates and processing](https://github.com/ChernStepanov/OffBeat/blob/master/docs/real-nisar.md) and [site manifests](https://github.com/ChernStepanov/OffBeat/tree/master/data) are available.

**Other sources:** ESA WorldCover 2021, Copernicus Sentinel-2 through Earth Search, and INPE/GLAD/FIRMS contextual records where individually identified. Retained sources and caveats are linked in the scientific review.
